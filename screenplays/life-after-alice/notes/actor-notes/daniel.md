# DANIEL: Actor's Notes

_For whoever plays Daniel in **LIFE AFTER ALICE**, human or synthetic. Playable notes. Draft for Tendai to push back on._

## One line

A man who refuses to rehearse being a widower, because rehearsing it makes it real.

## What he wants

For it to not be happening. Failing that, for her to stop making him practise.

## The obstacle

She won't stop. The notebook is everywhere: on her knee, at 2 a.m., in the therapist's hands.

## Tactics (what he's DOING)

1. **Refuse, lightly.** "I'm not putting logistics." Sarcastic, amused. This is a running bit between them and he's happy to play it.
2. **Rein her in.** "Al." Low, firm. He's embarrassed in front of a stranger.
3. **Defend his corner.** "That was ONCE." Frustrated and a bit petty. A real argument, not a tragic one.
4. **Joke so he doesn't have to answer.** "Not being here, I suppose." Quiet, half a joke, and it misfires.
5. **Undo her.** The highlighter lids. No words. Tired, 2 a.m., a man putting lids on things because he can't put a lid on this.
6. **Lash out, then backpedal.** "Coming in early" slips out sarcastic. He hears it instantly and scrambles: panicked, ashamed.
7. **Dig in.** "I'll do what I want. After." Shouted. The only time he raises his voice properly. Then the "After" comes out cold and quiet, and it's worse than the shout.
8. **Go frantic.** Heidelberg. Fast, breathless, a man trying to out-research death on his phone.
9. **Shut down.** Ruth's Tuesday. Short answers, flat, far away. "Standing in it."
10. **Hold on.** Kneeling, into her cardigan. Crying, stubborn, a little ridiculous. "I'll get another one."
11. **Fail to finish.** "And when it's—" He can't. Don't fill the gap.

## Voice signature

Black South London, early-to-mid thirties. A grounded, slightly rough voice. Talks fast when he's rattled, short when he's hurt. He answers the smaller question. He trails off rather than lands. His humour is sarcastic and quick, and he's actually funny.

## What NOT to play

- Not a hard man. Tough on the surface, but this is a man who cries into a cardigan.
- Not stoic or silent. He goes quiet only in the last scene, when he's run out.
- Not an older, settled husband. He's young and he's furious about it.
- Not tearful early. Nothing wet until the kneel.

## For the voice (table read)

**Voice description (for ElevenLabs Voice Design):**
"Black British man in his early thirties from South London, natural everyday London accent, grounded and slightly rough, conversational and quick, sarcastic sense of humour. Warm underneath. Not a narrator, not posh, not exaggerated slang."

**Baseline:** quick, conversational, a bit sardonic.

**Where he breaks:**
- "That was ONCE": frustrated.
- "That came out... I didn't mean—": panicked.
- "I'll do what I want. [pause] After.": shouted, then cold.
- Heidelberg: frantic, fast.
- The kneel: crying, muffled.
- "And when it's—": barely there.

**Tags he gets:** `[sarcastic]` `[frustrated]` `[nervous]` `[shouting]` `[tired]` `[quietly]` `[crying]` (kneel only) `[laughs]`.
**Tags he never gets:** `[sad]` as a baseline, stacked tags.
