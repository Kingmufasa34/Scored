# ALICE: Actor's Notes

_For whoever plays Alice in **LIFE AFTER ALICE**, human or synthetic. Playable notes, not backstory to perform. Draft for Tendai to push back on._

## One line

A woman who has run every project she's ever touched and is now running her own death like a work deadline, because a deadline is something she knows how to beat.

## What she wants

To leave Daniel ready. Every list, every tab, every "page fourteen" is her trying to hand him a life he can actually live without her. She can't fix the dying, so she fixes the boiler.

## The obstacle

He won't take it. Every time she hands him the plan, he hears it as her leaving.

## Tactics (what she's DOING)

1. **Tease.** Waiting room. "Put logistics." She's the funny one and she's on form. Playful, quick, a little smug.
2. **Run the meeting.** The agenda. Nervous, too fast, apologising and steamrolling in the same breath. She's scared of the room, so she chairs it.
3. **Hit back.** "I'm dying, I'm not thick." Hot and sharp, not tearful. She's angry at being handled.
4. **Deflect with a laugh.** "Item one's taken." She laughs too long because if she stops she'll have to hear what he said.
5. **Prosecute.** The bike, the flat tyres. Sarcastic and precise. She's good at this and she enjoys winning it a bit too much.
6. **Break.** The colander. The only time in the episode she loses control. It starts as instructions, fast and practical, and the crying ambushes her mid-word. She doesn't give in to it. It takes her.
7. **Perform fine.** "Same time next week?" Bright, brisk, slightly too loud. Coat half on. Everyone in the room can hear it's a performance, including her.

## Voice signature

Irish, early-to-mid thirties. Quick, warm, dry. She talks in complete sentences until she can't. Her humour is deadpan; she doesn't laugh at her own jokes, except once, too long. When she's frightened she gets faster and more organised, not quieter.

## What NOT to play

- Not frail. She's ill, not fading. Her voice is strong.
- Not sad before the colander. Annoyed, funny, nervous, angry, yes. Sad, no.
- Not sexy or breathy. Not a whisper-voice. Her warmth is in the wit, not the tone.
- Not middle-aged. She's young enough that this is outrageous.

## For the voice (table read)

**Voice description (for ElevenLabs Voice Design):**
"Irish woman in her early thirties from Dublin, natural conversational voice with a quick, dry, deadpan delivery. Warm but sharp, slightly husky, confident, speaks fast like a busy project manager. Not breathy, not soft-spoken, not a narrator."

**Baseline:** quick, dry, conversational. Unless a moment says otherwise, she sounds like she's mid-chat with someone she loves and finds slightly ridiculous.

**Where she breaks (and nowhere else):**
- "I'm dying, I'm not thick": angry, voice catching on the last word.
- "Item one's taken": the too-long laugh.
- "After what? No, go ON": angry and frightened.
- The colander: the one full cry.
- "Same time next week?": bright, a performance.

**Tags she gets:** `[playful]` `[sarcastic]` `[nervous]` `[angry]` `[laughs]` `[whispers]` (kitchen only) `[crying]` (colander only) `[excited]` (the door).
**Tags she never gets:** `[sad]`, `[softly]` as a baseline, stacked tags.
