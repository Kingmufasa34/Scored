# LIFE AFTER ALICE: Writer's Notes

_Guidance for any writer, actor or AI agent working on this series, including whoever directs the voices for the table read. It covers intent, subtext, what to preserve and what not to over-explain. Draft by Claude for Tendai to push back on. Nothing here is settled until Tendai says so._

---

## WHO THEY ARE (read this first)

Alice and Daniel are **in their thirties**. They have been together about eleven years, since their early twenties. That's long enough to have a colander with a fixed address and a bike with a flat tyre, and young enough that none of this was meant to happen yet.

They are **not a settled, worn-in middle-aged couple**. Don't play them as tired of each other or politely managing each other. They still wind each other up for fun. They still flirt by insulting each other. The waiting room is a couple bickering in the back of a cab, not a couple who've stopped talking.

Whether they are married is never said, and it doesn't need to be. They behave like partners. Nobody names it.

## Core concept

Alice is dying and is project-managing it. Daniel is losing her and is refusing to be project-managed.

It's not a cancer story. It's about two people with opposite ways of being frightened, stuck in a room with a stranger who's paid to watch.

- **Alice copes by planning.** If it's written down, it's handled. The notebook is love in the only form she trusts: lists, tabs and dates.
- **Daniel copes by refusing the future.** Every plan Alice makes is a rehearsal of her absence, so he won't engage with any of them. "I'll do what I want. After." His one act of defiance is refusing to be organised into widowhood.

Neither of them says "I'm scared". The scared comes out sideways: a joke, a highlighter lid, a fist on a vending machine.

## The central conflict

Alice: **"Let me leave you ready."**
Daniel: **"Don't make me rehearse it."**

Both are right. Every argument in the episode is this argument wearing a different object: a form, a boiler, highlighters, a bike, a colander.

## Tone

Funny first. The episode should get laughs in the first two minutes, real ones, before anyone cries. The comedy isn't relief from the grief; it's how these two carry it. Alice is the funniest person in any room she's in, and she knows it, and she uses it.

The sadness should mostly be **held back**, not poured out. One person cries properly in this episode (the colander), and it lands because nobody's been crying before it. If every line sounds sad, none of them is.

## Rhythm

- **Overlaps and interruptions.** They finish each other's sentences wrong. Dashes in the script are real cut-offs: the next line comes in on top.
- **Speed.** These two talk fast, the way people who've known each other a long time do. Ruth is the slow one in the room.
- **Silences are earned.** There are four or five real silences in the episode: after "I'm dying, I'm not thick", after "coming in early", "After what?", "Is the light on?" and "And when it's—". Everywhere else, keep it moving.

## The scenes, as temperature

1. **Waiting room.** Light. Bickering. A couple who are good at being a couple. Nobody's sad yet.
2. **Ruth's room.** Alice nervous and too fast, overcompensating with the agenda. One spike ("I'm dying, I'm not thick"), said hot, not tearful. Then Daniel's joke-that-isn't, and Alice laughs too long.
3. **Kitchen, 2 a.m.** Whispered, sleepy, intimate. Annoyed in the way you are at 2 a.m. Not tragic: it's two people who sleep in the same bed.
4. **Ruth's room.** A real row. Sarcasm escalating to shouting. They're good at hurting each other because they know exactly where to press. Then silence.
5. **Hospital corridor.** Daniel frantic, Alice flat and exhausted. Then the vending machine. Then the laughter, which is the worst sound in the episode.
6. **Ruth's room.** Quiet. Daniel shut down. Alice cracks at the colander, the only full break in the episode. Then tenderness, then a small joke ("I know"), then Alice's brightness at the door, which is a performance. Daniel can't finish his sentence.

## What not to do

- Don't play it sad all the way through. Play it funny, then let it break.
- Don't let anyone cry before the colander. Tears early cost the colander everything.
- Don't make Ruth wise. She's kind, a bit flustered, and out of her depth like anyone would be.
- Don't let Daniel be a stoic, silent man. He talks a lot, too fast, when he's frightened (Heidelberg). He only goes quiet when there's nothing left to say.
- Don't explain the notebook. Don't explain the scar.

## The emotional thesis

You can't plan someone through losing you. You can only leave the light on.
